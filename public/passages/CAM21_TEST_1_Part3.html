Listening Script – Section 3

Part 3. You will hear a conversation between two students, Jake and Mia, who are preparing a presentation on loneliness. First you have some time to look at questions twenty-one to twenty-six.

Now listen carefully and answer questions twenty-one to twenty-six.

Jake: So for our presentation, we need to talk about the causes of loneliness in modern society.
Mia: Right. I think urban design plays a huge role – cities are just not designed for social connection anymore.
Jake: Definitely. And a mobile workforce too – people keep moving for jobs, so they lose their established communities.
Mia: What about social media? I know a lot of people blame that.
Jake: I'm not convinced it's a cause rather than a symptom. I'd stick with urban design and the mobile workforce.
Mia: OK, agreed. Now for health risks – which ones do we have strong evidence for?
Jake: The research on the immune system is quite solid – loneliness clearly weakens immune function.
Mia: And cancer – there's growing evidence there too.
Jake: Yes, but dementia and cardiovascular disease – the evidence is more mixed.
Mia: Right, so we'll focus on immune system and cancer as having solid evidence.
Jake: Now, what about the evolutionary theory?
Mia: I think it has little practical relevance for modern life, even if it's interesting theoretically.
Jake: I agree. And it clearly needs more investigation – the evidence is still quite thin.

Before you hear the rest of the conversation, you have some time to look at questions twenty-seven to thirty.

Now listen and answer questions twenty-seven to thirty.

Mia: When we compare loneliness to depression, what's the key point?
Jake: I think the big issue is that, unlike depression, we doubt there will ever be a medical cure for loneliness. It's not a chemical imbalance.
Mia: Good point. Now, for our introduction – should we start with the statistics or our own experience?
Jake: Let's start with something personal. I think using our own experience will highlight a situation most students will recognise.
Mia: Right, everyone's felt lonely at some point. Good idea.
Jake: And for strategies – we're recommending talking to strangers?
Mia: Yes. The research shows it creates a sense of belonging, even brief interactions with strangers.
Jake: And finally, solitude – why do people enjoy it?
Mia: That's what puzzles me. I understand needing time alone, but genuinely finding it enjoyable – I find that hard to understand.
Jake: Me too. It seems so different from loneliness, yet some people actively seek it.

That is the end of Part 3. You now have thirty seconds to check your answers to Part 3.
