Listening Script – Section 2

Part 2. You will hear a talk by Heather at Edelman Pottery. First you have some time to look at questions eleven to sixteen.

Now listen and answer questions eleven to sixteen.

Hello, everyone, and welcome to Edelman Pottery. I'm Heather, and I'll be your guide today.

Now, why is pottery different from other art forms? Well, one of the most remarkable things about pottery is that it lasts longer in the ground than almost any other material. This is why it's so valuable to archaeologists.

Speaking of archaeologists – they often identify what ancient pottery was used for by looking at the marks on it. Those marks reveal so much about daily life thousands of years ago.

Some of you have mentioned why you decided to join this class. It's interesting that many people come because they want to create something that will outlive them – something permanent that could still exist long after they're gone.

And why do I love being a potter? For me, it's the calming effect. Working with clay has a wonderfully meditative quality that I find deeply peaceful.

Now, most of our visitors at Edelman Pottery have never made a pot before – and that's completely fine! We welcome all levels.

Just a quick reminder before we start – please take off your jewellery. Rings and bracelets can get stuck in the clay and it can be quite difficult to remove them once you start.

Before you hear the rest of the talk, you have some time to look at questions seventeen to twenty.

Now listen and answer questions seventeen to twenty.

Now let me tell you about kilns. A kiln is essentially a high-temperature oven for firing pottery. What does a kiln do? Well, the heat transforms the clay permanently – that's its function. And interestingly, not everyone has access to a kiln. Some potters use a technique called pit firing as an alternative – digging a pit in the ground and using that instead of a conventional kiln.

And finally, let me say a few words about potter's tools. There are many tools available in our studio. Some of them are absolutely essential – you simply cannot make pottery without them. And the great news is that all of our tools are available for participants to use during the session – you don't need to bring anything.

Thank you everyone, let's begin!
