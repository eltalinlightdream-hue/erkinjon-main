Listening Script – Section 3

Part 3. You will hear a conversation between two students, Laura and Marcus, who are discussing children's handwriting research. First you have some time to look at questions twenty-one to twenty-six.

Laura: Marcus, I've been reading all this research on handwriting. Some findings really surprised me.
Marcus: Like what?
Laura: Well, I knew handwriting helped with reading, but I didn't expect it to improve concentration. Apparently children who write by hand focus much better.
Marcus: That is surprising. And spatial awareness – I was shocked by that. Writing helps develop spatial thinking in ways that typing doesn't.
Laura: Exactly. Now, for children with dyspraxia – the coordination difficulties – what problems can actually be corrected?
Marcus: The spacing issue – not putting enough space between letters – seems quite fixable with practice.
Laura: And applying too much pressure. Apparently that's trainable too with the right exercises.
Marcus: Right. Whereas things like not forming letters properly or holding the pen incorrectly are much harder to address.
Laura: Now, laptops for dyspraxic children – what do you think?
Marcus: The research suggests children actually respond more positively to mistakes when using laptops. They don't get as discouraged.

Before you hear the rest of the conversation, you have some time to look at questions twenty-seven to thirty.

Laura: What about cursive writing versus print?
Marcus: Some argue cursive is harder for dyslexic children.
Laura: I think there's genuine evidence that cursive disadvantages certain groups. It's not just a theory.
Marcus: And for exams – does handwriting quality affect grades?
Laura: There is evidence – studies show grades are affected by poor handwriting, even when content is identical.
Marcus: That's concerning. For the future – I think fewer people will be able to write neatly. It's becoming a lost skill.
Laura: And for exam performance – I think candidates will write less, producing shorter answers as writing by hand becomes more difficult.
Marcus: I feel quite guilty about my own handwriting actually. I used to have lovely handwriting as a child.
Laura: Me too. I really regret losing that habit. I almost never write anything by hand now.

That is the end of Part 3. You now have thirty seconds to check your answers to Part 3.
