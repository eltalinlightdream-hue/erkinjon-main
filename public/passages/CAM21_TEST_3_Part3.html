Listening Script – Section 3

Finn: I'm so glad we chose this topic. I was really pleased when I found out no other students had picked it.

Maya: One common misconception is that theatres actually pay to have programmes produced. Actually, it's the opposite – companies pay the theatres.

Finn: In early British theatre, programmes were actually seen as a kind of contract between the theatre and the audience.

Maya: We should explain in our project why acting companies used to promote their own plays through programmes.

Finn: Eighteenth-century programmes were more informative than nineteenth-century ones.

Maya: In Britain, theatre programmes failed to develop for so long – I don't understand why.

Maya: Ruy Blas – visually extraordinary, resembles a piece of artwork. Man of La Mancha – detailed insights into the show. The Tragedy of Jane Shore – historically significant, first examples printed for a British play. The Sailors' Festival – has been included in a recent museum project.
